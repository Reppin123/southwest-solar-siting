# Independent methodology review: Southwest solar siting

**Reviewed 2026-10-21.** Scope: the four agents' scripts, processed outputs and manifests in the read-only `../southwest-solar-siting` project, its `SCOPE.md`, the BLM brief and sources list, and the *planned/in-progress* screening code. This is a review of suitability for choosing 100 MW to 1 GW solar and optional 50 to 500 MW load, not an engineering feasibility study or legal opinion. Severity: **blocking** means a result must not be used to recommend a parcel as feasible; **important** means it can materially reorder sites or misstate costs; **minor** means documentation or secondary precision. Counts below are my independent arithmetic on existing CSVs/manifests using `review/checks/audit.py`, run with `/opt/homebrew/bin/uv run --script review/checks/audit.py`. I did not run screening, rebuild data, or modify the source project. An individual substation count reference independent of the HIFLD source could not be verified.

## 1. Grid agent: lines, substations, interconnection queues

**Solid.** `pipeline/process_grid.py:125-155,195-262` clips lines to TIGER 2024 state polygons, assigns substation state/county, works in EPSG:5070 for lengths and distance matching, distinguishes raw, OSM-matched and line-inferred kV, retains full GPKG geometry, and deduplicates HIFLD IDs. Its 9,086 line *pieces* cover all five states; 7,883 substation *points* include 7,553 HIFLD and 330 OSM, not all confirmed interconnection facilities. `process_grid.py:286-389` retains withdrawn, suspended and operational queue statuses separately, preserves component solar/storage MW, and reconciles solar components to the Queued Up workbook's state solar totals (ratios 1.0 in `queue_state_check.csv`). LBNL Queued Up 2026 through December 2025 and CAISO's report are appropriate public starting points.

- **[blocking] Nearby equipment is not an available POI.** `process_grid.py:211-256` admits OSM sites by a 1,500 m nearest-HIFLD rule and infers max kV from any line within 200 m. A plant collector substation, line crossing, closed switchyard, or wrong circuit can therefore appear suitable. `data/processed/grid/MANIFEST.json` says 330 OSM additions, 180 HIFLD voltages inferred from lines, 83 matched from OSM, and 279 still unknown. Explicitly restrict candidates to operating utility-owned transmission POIs, validate operator, bus/line topology, terminal equipment and voltage with utility one-lines, and rank unverified sites as provisional. Compare substation totals against utility asset inventories by territory; no authoritative cross-state comparison was supplied.
- **[important] Queue geography overstates precision.** `process_grid.py:342-350,392-397` matches POI names in-state, rejects names with hyphens/line/tap and writes zero for unmatched `queue_active_mw`. My CSV count: only **508/1,191 active queue requests** have a `poi_sub_id`; 683 have no matched POI. Zero is **unknown**, not an empty queue. Use normalized utility POI IDs, line/tap geometries and study cluster, and show matching coverage and confidence. `screen.py` uses county MW instead (see section 5), which is not congestion at the nearest bus.
- **[important] Scope is not the promised full queue coverage.** `pipeline/fetch_grid.py:15-17,57-59` fetches LBNL and CAISO, not live LADWP, IID, SRP, APS, TEP, PNM, NV Energy or PacifiCorp queue files listed in `SCOPE.md:23`. `process_grid.py:307-315` only fills blank LBNL POI/county from CAISO; LBNL statuses are not refreshed from CAISO `caiso_sheet`. Request current provider lists, cluster status, withdrawal milestones, study results, transmission upgrade costs and large-load requests separately; identify source timestamp per project.
- **[important] Nameplate MW is not interchangeable with transmission service requested.** `process_grid.py:327-329,362-367` sums `mw_1` through `mw_3` into county `active_mw_total`. My audit finds **399,266.3 MW** active total; solar components **176,653.1 MW**, storage **161,400.5 MW**; 536 active hybrid rows account for **246,672.3 MW** of total. The county totals reconcile exactly to active project MW, so there is no row duplication in that aggregation, but solar and storage components in a hybrid should not be read as independent simultaneous export or as extra projects. Avoid adding total + solar + storage or the type=hybrid group again; keep requested injection MW, charging load and hybrid shared-POI limits distinct. Storage components are underreported relative to LBNL state maps: `queue_state_check.csv` storage ratios AZ .480, CA .872, NM .397, NV .478, UT .457; explain imputation before interpreting state differences.
- **[important] Source age and classification.** `data/raw/grid/hifld_substations/_complete.json` has `dataLastEditDate=1614287020427`, **2021-02-25 UTC**, despite 2026 retrieval. The HIFLD line mirror's edit date is not proof every line is current. `process_grid.py:73-84,139-154` bins 162-299 kV as `230`, 300-499 as `345`, and 500+ together; 577/9,086 line pieces (**6.35%**) and 279/7,883 substations (**3.54%**) are unknown kV. Bins are labels, not nominal equipment voltage. Publish a cross-tab of exact nominal voltages, unknowns, status and state, exclude unknown and non-operating from high-voltage scoring, and cross-check recent utility planning maps, WECC and EIA transmission references where usable. OSM requires attribution and share-alike obligations for a derivative database; verify HIFLD mirror permission and document provenance before distributing maps.

**Verdict: 6/10** as reconnaissance, not interconnection feasibility.

## 2. Land agent: BLM, ownership, exclusions, terrain, resource

**Solid.** `pipeline/process_land.py:318-404` actually reads the BLM 2024 ROD geodatabase `Solar_PEIS_ROD_Approved_Plan` with `ApprovedPlan='Available'`, not a generic buffer proxy; treats California DRECP DFA and variance separately and retains SEZ developable/non-developable status (26 SEZ records). `process_land.py:189-225,410-445` clips polygons and computes acres in EPSG:5070, and full-resolution GPKGs accompany simplified GeoJSONs. `ghi.tif` uses the archived NREL NSRDB PSM v3 1998-2016 annual average daily GHI; manifest states **kWh/m2/day**, source pixels about 0.04 degrees (~4 km), not annual kWh or site-grade yield. Internet Archive is a copy of an older NREL file, not a new measurement; archive URL, original metadata and checksum should travel with the deliverable. A current NSRDB/Solargis-independent public check such as NREL NSRDB recent-year samples or Global Solar Atlas is useful for top sites, subject to their license/coverage.

- **[blocking] BLM legal availability is not demonstrated for every scored BLM acre.** `process_land.py:330-360` dissolves ROD Available by state then appends DRECP DFA *and variance*; category names survive, but `blm_solar_available=True` is set on all, including variance (line 361). The GIS availability footprint is not intersected with current surface management, withdrawals or encumbrances at production. The screen must require a current BLM surface-rights intersection and parcel-level plan designation, and separate DFA, variance, ROD Available, avoidance and SEZ statuses rather than a universal boolean. Verify the 2024 ROD's 'Avoidance' classification against the brief's claim it is available; this script selects only `ApprovedPlan='Available'`, so if eligible avoidance is separate, it is omitted. Do not infer that all 26 SEZ geometry records are 26 distinct developable zones.
- **[important] Simplified map exclusions are unsuitable as hard screening masks.** `process_land.py:251-286,460-475` can remove holes and parts to meet a 24 MB GeoJSON budget; `exclusion_flood_zone_a` uses server-generalized ~30 m FEMA geometry and incomplete NFHL coverage (`:525-537`). Screening should use the full GPKG and mark FEMA unmapped places *unknown*, not flood-free, with field flood hazard review and wash mapping. `screen.py` appears to use both full and map copies, but its output was not verified in this review.
- **[important] Hard/soft choices need jurisdiction-specific logic.** `process_land.py:498-579` makes ACEC soft despite its own caveat that Western Solar Plan excludes ACECs on BLM, and NWI wetlands >=40 acres soft, omitting smaller waters and washes. USFWS final critical habitat is a prudent screening hard constraint, not a universal legal prohibition on private land; species presence extends beyond it. Restrict BLM to applicable plan polygons and actual resource-designation restrictions; add separate BLM, private and state policies and specialist review rather than labeling every designation a legal exclusion.
- **[important] Resolution and land titles.** `process_land.py:681-808` derives slope from AWS Terrarium z11 (~62 m ground pixels at 35N), averages to ~90 m, and its hard slope mask excludes only >=100-acre patches where >=50% of ~0.005-degree cells exceed 5%. Small steep pitches and access grades are retained. A 5% screen is conservative relative to the brief's cited 10% Western Solar Plan slope exclusion, but neither proves engineering buildability; rerun top sites on direct USGS 3DEP elevation with 10 m or best local DEM and quantify area-weighted distribution, drainage and grading. `process_land.py:407-445` maps SMA `UND` to Private and `ST` to State (not confirmed trust ownership); SMA is administration, not cadastral title. Pull state trust cadasters and county parcel/title data before calling any block obtainable.
- **[minor] Licensing and source vintage.** Census TIGER, BLM, USFWS and FEMA have government open-data origins; the NREL file via Internet Archive and AWS Terrain Tiles are redistributed copies/services. Record specific attribution/redistribution terms, originals and hashes; do not assert 2026 vintage for 1998-2016 GHI or for a 2023 basin model.

**Verdict: 6/10** for regional overlay; parcel availability and terrain remain provisional.

## 3. Infrastructure agent: generation, fiber, water, access, BLM cases

**Solid.** `pipeline/process_infra.py:147-267` carries generator-level 2025 EIA-860 rows and rolls operating, proposed, retired and canceled separately, with 3,025 geocoded plant points and 5,989 generator rows (`MANIFEST.json`). Its `power_plants` layer includes retired sites rather than silently deleting them, useful for reuse ideas. TIGER 2025 primary roads are clipped in EPSG:4326 and distances in EPSG:5070; both full GPKGs and lighter map copies exist. Aqueduct 4.0 is clearly labeled basin-scale 1979-2019 baseline; AZ AMA/INA layer is separately available.

- **[important] Plant status/technology is a lossy plant-level reduction.** `process_infra.py:159-210` reads all three EIA sheets; it uses any operable generator (including standby) for operating MW and selects `tech` using operable **plus proposed** MW even when status is operating. A plant with a proposed solar addition can be classified solar despite its installed MW being gas. `mw` is nameplate, not firm export or spare interconnection. Separate existing vs proposed fuel/class and avoid using an EIA point as the occupied footprint or spare grid headroom; compare operating-capacity totals to EIA's state summaries.
- **[blocking for data center ranking] Fiber geometry is mostly not fiber.** `process_infra.py:309-381` concatenates OSM telecom lines, interstate and Class I rail corridors. Independent manifest audit: **9/1,105 features** explicitly tagged fiber; **1,096/1,105 (99.19%)** are proxies or medium-unknown telecom. A nearest-distance calculation that treats all equally can put a candidate beside a rail line with no service. Split confirmed fiber, unverified telecom and transport-corridor opportunity into separate columns; require carrier route/service letter, dark-fiber availability, redundancy, latency and lateral cost before awarding DC points. FCC location-level broadband availability may help cross-check service, but is not a route or high-capacity fiber guarantee.
- **[important] Water and access are not site-level feasibility.** `process_infra.py:393-459` uses 583 Aqueduct basin x state records and `water_stress=bws_score` (0 to 5). This does not establish permitted groundwater withdrawals, potable/reclaimed supply, drought yield or cooling demand. `process_infra.py:270-305` uses TIGER *primary roads only*, not drivable parcel access. Add utility water supply/rights and cooling scenarios; check local roads, grade, bridges and easements. Include industrial zoning, load service tariff and utility territory for data centers.
- **[important] MLRS cases are neither complete footprints nor all real projects.** `process_infra.py:480-557` fetches solar-commodity case geometries; **854 rows**, **349 labeled active**, **47 without geometry**. Of the 349 active, **278** are Solar Development Grant and **16** Solar Development Lease; others include test, transmission and even wind types. Active GeoJSON has **307** features (42 active records have no mapped geometry by difference), so screening overlaps miss them. Geometry is often whole PLSS sections, not construction footprint. Use case serial number and legal descriptions, current MLRS search plus field office inquiry, and classify authorization, proposal, test and non-solar separately; do not equate an intersecting polygon with an occupied site or no intersection with no conflict.

**Verdict: 6/10** for context, **not** a qualified data center interconnection/fiber/water screen.

## 4. BLM brief: accuracy, legal and commercial risk

**Solid.** `deliverables/BLM_Application_Brief.md` distinguishes available land from approved ROW (lines 43-65), identifies DRECP special handling, gives a concrete field-office and utility checklist, and states open items instead of silently treating all 2026 policy as settled. `deliverables/sources_blm.md` lists primary and secondary sources and access caveats. I independently opened the following *cited official texts* on 2026-10-21; results are spot-checks, not a complete legal audit:

| Brief claim / location | Official text independently checked | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 ROD effective after 2024-12-19 signing, lines 43-45 | [Federal Register 2024-30953](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2024-30953), DATES | Supported. Plan effectiveness does **not** guarantee that a particular case can be filed or approved today. |
| SF-299, $15/acre, two preliminary meetings within 6 months of cost-recovery payment, lines 92-115, 201-202 | [43 CFR 2804.12(a)-(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-43/part-2800/section-2804.12) | Supported; GIS shapefiles are required **when requested**, not invariably, and the rule says *schedule and hold* both meetings. |
| Bond at least $10,000 per disturbed acre, lines 205, 92-115 | [43 CFR 2805.20(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-43/part-2800/section-2805.20) | Supported for solar **grants outside designated leasing areas**, based on reclamation estimate; must not generalize to every ROW/data center. |
| Greater of acreage rent or 3.9% gross electricity proceeds, lines 15, 122-143 | [43 CFR 2806.52](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-43/part-2800/section-2806.52) | Supported for solar/wind development; proration applies to public-land footprint. It is a *capacity fee*, not an across-the-board rent on a data center's output. |
| New fee effective 'since 2025-07-04', line 15 | [BLM IM 2026-003](https://www.blm.gov/policy/im-2026-003), Policy/Action and Prorate Billing | **Needs correction**: statute dated July 4, but BLM instructs billing at new rates effective **August 1, 2025**, with prorated 2025 invoices. Distinguish enactment from billed effective date. |
| March 15 gross-proceeds reporting and April 15 bills, section 6 | [BLM IM 2026-003](https://www.blm.gov/policy/im-2026-003), Capacity Fee | Supported as BLM reporting request and *anticipated* bill date, not guaranteed issuance on April 15. |
| Data-center EO qualifies load over 100 MW **or** $500M, lines 31, 176-188 | [EO 14318 / FR 2025-14212](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2025-14212), section 2 | Partly supported: a **Data Center Project** is defined as AI-dedicated *greater than 100 MW* new load; qualifying project can alternatively be a data-center or covered-component project with >=$500M sponsor capex, national-security basis, or official designation. EO does not say solar-only supply **cannot** benefit, only that solar is not listed in the dispatchable-baseload examples. |

- **[blocking for investment advice] Tax-credit certainty exceeds primary-source check.** `BLM_Application_Brief.md:16,34,38,253` says a newly filed BLM project *gets no solar ITC/PTC* and instructs modeling without credits. This is reasonable as a conservative scenario but combines an assumed construction schedule with a statutory/tax-law conclusion sourced to trade and law-firm summaries. Independently verify enacted tax text, Treasury/IRS beginning-construction guidance, grandfathering, equipment and foreign-entity rules, and sponsor facts with tax counsel; model both credit and no-credit sensitivities. Do not make eligibility a categorical finding for all projects.
- **[important] Injunction characterization needs docket check.** Lines 14,35,199,256 rely on a law-firm summary for scope of a preliminary injunction and infer nonmember companies remain under the memo. I could not verify the actual court order, association membership, appeal or subsequent modification from the cited *official* record. Recast as a reported injunction subject to counsel's direct docket review; never recommend joining an association as a shortcut without legal advice.
- **[important] Overstated EO and process language.** Line 31 concludes solar-only power will not get special treatment, which the EO text does not establish. Lines 196-201 mention 'BLM-only' parcel toggle and utility headroom despite no confirmed headroom dataset. Label that a due-diligence goal, not a verified input. California DRECP **variance** lands merit separate risk treatment rather than shorthand 'outside DFA' in line 251.
- **[minor] Citation hygiene.** Section 9 makes some state trust fees/timing claims from snippets behind HTTP 403 and section 2 relies on an advocacy account for a data center conversion. Keep these as unverified leads, not filing instructions. Validate 2026 rent tables and official order texts at execution time.

**Verdict: 7/10** as a question-and-process brief; **not** a substitute for counsel or a budget-ready fee/tax opinion.

## 5. Screening method (planned / code present, outputs not assessed)

`SCOPE.md:29-41` and `TEAM/briefs/screening.md` prescribe 1 km EPSG:5070 cells, hard exclusions, >=600 contiguous acres, factor weights 30 grid / 25 land / 15 sun / 15 permit / 15 DC. `pipeline/screen.py` existed at inspection with a documented `WEIGHTS` dictionary at lines 55-109 and a proposed exact-geometry hard-exclusion clip. I **did not run it or validate site, Excel or memo outputs**, so findings here are on the method/code, not claims about finished rankings.

**Solid.** Metric projected area/distance, explicit exponential decay and tracked factor weights make the method auditable. Separating a full-resolution final exclusion clip from raster first-pass and planning 600+ acre contiguous polygons is more defensible than centroid-only checks. 600 acres at 5-7 acres/MW is about **86-120 MW**, so it is an initial ~100 MW threshold, **not** a 1 GW threshold (which needs ~5,000-7,000 usable acres before buffers and auxiliary facilities).

- **[blocking] Need two distinct decision tracks and feasibility gates.** `screen.py:55-62` sums five factors including DC at 15% for every site, although `SCOPE.md:38` says DC only for the DC track. A weak fiber proxy can reorder solar-only projects. Produce solar-only weights renormalized to 100 (e.g. 30/85, 25/85, 15/85, 15/85), separate solar+DC rank, and gate on legal BLM status, usable contiguous acreage at target MW, point-of-interconnection and load-service pathway. A high score must never override a failed feasibility gate.
- **[blocking] False queue and grid precision.** `screen.py:55-69` scores nearest 115 kV+ substation (40% of grid score), nearest 230 kV+ line (20%), voltage (20%), county queue GW (20%, 15 GW exponential scale). `screen.py` assigns county total from `queue_by_county.csv`, not the nearest substation's queue. For a county with 15 GW, the queue term becomes 36.8/100 regardless of circuit or utility; county boundary discontinuities and a 0-MW unmatched POI are not evidence of headroom. Require utility-service territory, line owner, deliverability/transfer-capacity and expansion proxies (planning studies, thermal/voltage constraints, upgrade timelines), distinguish generator export from coincident DC load, and make queue a low-confidence sensitivity variable rather than capacity.
- **[important] Nonlinear weights are policy choices, not calibration.** `screen.py:65-109` chooses 15 km substation and 20 km line exponential scales, flat voltage steps, 10 km fiber scale, owner base BLM=20 vs Private=70 plus BLM bonus=65, and mean-slope/5 penalty. At 15 km substation distance the score drops to 36.8; at zero km an inaccessible bus still scores 100. Show sensitivity to +/-50% decay lengths and plausible weights; check top-25 stability and report score component intervals for uncertain measurements. Model actual gen-tie route length, ROW crossing, terrain and cost per MW instead of straight-line distance.
- **[important] Raster/ownership boundary uncertainty.** `screen.py` first permits cells with <50% hard area and <50% steep pixels, then proposes exact clipping. Confirm after clipping that each resulting block stays >=600 acres of actual usable land, ownership categories are coherent, steep patches/drainages do not sever buildable connectivity, and no hard intersection survives. Test buffer/geometry tolerances against full original datasets, not only simplified copies. Record missing raster/ownership coverage as unknown, not an innocuous score.
- **[important] Economic and permitting factors omitted.** No land price/lease bid, interconnection upgrades, ROW crossing and corridors, environmental mitigation, construction road improvements, county zoning, tax exposure or utility permitting schedule. Add these to a second-stage cost and timeline model, with separate solar vs DC scenarios and confidence grading. SEZs and ROW corridors are prioritization cues, not permits; BLM available areas are not exclusive site control.

**Provisional verdict: 5/10** as a transparent candidate generator; no numerical site ranking has been independently validated.

## Overall verdict and ranked top 10 fixes (ranked by likely effect on site rankings)

| Task | Score /10 | Decision use now |
|---|---:|---|
| Grid | 6 | Regional proximity, not verified capacity |
| Land | 6 | Regional land screen, not parcel/title determination |
| Infrastructure | 6 | Context and flags, not DC service proof |
| BLM brief | 7 | Meeting preparation, not legal/tax opinion |
| Screening method (provisional) | 5 | Hypothesis generation only |

1. **Verify POI, utility territory, line owner and realistic export plus load interconnection path**, with utility pre-application and queue study data; gate candidates before scoring.
2. **Separate solar-only and solar+DC rankings**; do not give proxy fiber and regional water 15% of the solar-only ranking.
3. **Intersect BLM designation with current BLM surface rights, SEZ/avoidance/variance and actual encumbrances**; reject legally ineligible or conflicting parcels.
4. **Confirm contiguous *usable* area for the target MW**, using high-resolution DEM, wash/habitat setbacks and 5,000-7,000+ usable acres when assessing ~1 GW.
5. **Replace county queue congestion and unmatched zero POIs** with utility/cluster/POI status, confidence and separate generation vs load requests.
6. **Distinguish confirmed carrier fiber from transport corridors**, secure redundancy and lateral quotes before DC scoring.
7. **Correct ownership and state trust parcel status** against cadasters, deeds, mineral severance and lease terms; obtain land price/lease bids.
8. **Validate environmental and flood exclusions locally**, including unmapped NFHL counties, small wetlands/washes, cultural resources, ACECs and species outside critical habitat.
9. **Add gen-tie and ROW corridor, access, substation upgrade, permit and schedule costs** to a ranked financial/timeline sensitivity model.
10. **Audit score sensitivity, timestamps and licenses**, publish uncertainty intervals and rerank with current utility queues, direct 3DEP and updated irradiance.

## Offline verification checklist before trusting *any* site

- [ ] Download and archive source snapshots with license/attribution, hashes, retrieval date and original vintage; trace each scored field to a raw record and CRS. Confirm HIFLD mirror rights, OSM attribution/ODbL, archived NREL and terrain service terms.
- [ ] GIS analyst verifies legal parcel boundary, SMA versus title, state trust status, rights/withdrawals, mining/grazing/ROW interests and field-office land-use designation, including CA DRECP and avoidance/variance treatment.
- [ ] Recompute acreage from full-resolution EPSG:5070 geometries; test connectivity after setbacks, washes and exclusion holes. Verify at least the target-MW-specific *usable* acres, not only 600 gross acres.
- [ ] Recheck critical habitat, occupied species habitat, wetlands/waters, ACECs, cultural and tribal consultation, FEMA coverage status, local flood/wash maps, and military/airspace constraints with agencies and surveys.
- [ ] Verify exact bus/line voltage, status, owner, utility jurisdiction and likely tie route against utility diagrams and field conditions; ask for capacity/upgrade cost, study cluster, site-control requirement and dispatch/export constraints in writing.
- [ ] For co-location, submit *both* generation and large-load service scenarios, resource adequacy/back-up and utility tariffs; obtain independent carrier fiber letters, diverse routes and lateral pricing, water rights/supply and cooling design.
- [ ] Quote land acquisition or lease, reclamation bond, ROW fees and rent, upgrade and gen-tie, road works, tax scenarios and NEPA/permitting timeline; check current 43 CFR 2800, DOI instructions and litigation docket with counsel.
- [ ] Reproduce top-site rank under alternate weights/decay and missing-data assumptions, compare scores by state and utility territory, and do not mark an unknown value as zero risk. Check actual `candidate_sites` hard-overlap and Excel formulas **after screening completes**.

**Bottom line:** useful first-pass evidence, but the present datasets do not prove power headroom, BLM entitlement, water/fiber service or buildable acreage for a named project. No investment or filing recommendation should rely on the composite score alone.
